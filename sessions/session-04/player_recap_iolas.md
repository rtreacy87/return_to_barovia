
Return to Barovia, Session 4 recap
–
And so we found ourselves in the dungeons of Castle Ravenloft. Unbothered for the time being, yes, but feeling none the safer for it, as we were in the midst of one vampire and several creatures I can only describe as ghoul-like. I can bear to give them no more of my mind than that.
Fortunately, the ghouls were unmoving and the vampire, similarly, seemed to be enveloped in some sort of trance. This gave us a welcome opportunity; Tivwick, who seems to have never heard the tale of the cat and its troublesome curiosity, cast a hasting spell on Ismark, allowing him to make a flurry of attacks with his newly discovered Vorpal Sword and dispatch of the vampire before it ever had a chance to respond. Perhaps not the fairest of fights, but we need not make apologies in this realm.
The party then spread out among the chamber and Kethri decided to explore the primary area. After a moment, she reported that there were several deep slash marks on the ground, with an appearance that suggested the presence of a large creature. At the time, this discovery felt foreboding; shortly after, it proved to be.
In the meantime, I took hold of the keys found on the vampire’s corpse and opened the door that it seemed to have been guarding. As the door squealed open on ancient, rusty hinges, I was hit with odors of blood, sweat and urine, telltale signs of the suffering of the figure hidden in shadow within the final cell at the end of the corridor. I approached, with several of the party in my wake, and unlocked the crude manacles binding a figure doused in blood and grime. As he looked up at me, I recognized a face that looked twenty years older than I expected it to be—my former traveling companion, Ubral.
I also noticed that many of his wounds seemed much older and further along the healing process than they should be in only eleven days. Is this some magic come from Ubral’s bond to Barovia, or is there perhaps something darker at play? Only time will tell.
Ubral immediately asked for water, in a voice strained with pain and disuse. He slowly and gingerly sipped from my waterskin, then said “You came. Perhaps Helm has not abandoned us after all.” We allowed him the grace of several more minutes, then asked for his account of what had happened in this place. He replied, “The enemy possessed military precision and attacked with ruthless brutality. They sought something ancient and hidden but ultimately left empty-handed.”
Ubral’s eyes began to move around the room, first settling on Ismark. “I know what’s left of your face,” the cleric rasped, pointing a thick finger in his direction. Ismark responded simply, “Your people put me in a cage.” Seeming to be genuinely dismayed, Ubral asked for more information on how Ismark came to be imprisoned. Ismark recounted his capture and imprisonment to the best of his ability, but there seem to be many gaps yet to be filled in that tale.
Dagomir then spoke up and told Ismark that he is the rightful Burgomaster of Barovia. “This is true,” Ubral replied. “You come from an honored bloodline but you are tainted.”
“Ubral and I bear responsibility for your fate,” I added. I feel great shame at remembering my role in what befell Ismark and now that shame is greater, as I have avoided confronting that past and revealing to Ismark his own identity and past. I know I must do this, and soon; I only hope that I can muster the courage to be honest with him and that he will be merciful in offering me forgiveness, whether I deserve it or not.
Aldric, on the other hand, was nearly reverent in Ubral’s presence. Ubral asked how he came to find himself with the rest of our party (or “this lot” as he referred to us) but Aldric declined to answer, saying only, “It’s a long story but I’m glad to see you alive.”
Ubral responded, “Well, at any rate, we will need someone pure of heart, such as a follower of the Morninglord.” Aldric then stepped forward, presumably to heal some of Ubral’s wounds but Ubral suddenly backed away. I have no reason to distrust the man, but he almost seemed to be hiding something. Curious.
Ubral’s man Dagomir proceeded to pull his master’s armor from a nearby wall and began dressing him. Ubral’s condition seemed to improve almost immediately and he took a knee to pray. Some things even Barovia can’t force to change, I suppose. And although I don’t share Ubral’s penchant for prayer, there was something oddly comforting about that being a constant in this harsh and unforgiving land.
Ubral then continued to look around and his eyes fell next upon Gwynlian, seemingly bringing the Vistani to mind. “The Vistani rode west to the Summer Court,” he told her. “Some of my men followed them, but they never returned.” A pause, then, “We must reach Vallaki, even though House Watcher has no love for Helm.”
Gwynlian unsheathed her tarokka cards. I could sense Ubral’s unease at this, but he remained silent. Gwynlian’s eyes suddenly went white and she went into some sort of trance. After several minutes, she dropped her cards to the floor and relayed to us what she had seen:
—
The Artifact. A king with a ruined throne sits upon a longship under a black sky. He holds a sword—the artifact itself. It is darker than shadow, older even than the kingdom of which Barovia was once a part. This is what the vampires seek.
The Marionette. Music, laughter and firelight fill the air as Vistani dance around a roaring bonfire. Fiddles play and wine flows. Within the crowd of revelers is a monk, although it is not Kethri or Rød. Rippling banners bear a blazing heraldic sun, but something is wrong. While the singing and dancing continues, the people cry and a woman says “Help us.” On the horizon lies Krezk and the monastery it holds.
—
Gwynlian began sobbing, as she said “We must go to Krezk and help my people.”
After a short discussion, we came to agreement that the artifact in Gwynlian’s vision was most likely what the vampires were seeking and that it may have resided here in Castle Ravenloft at one time. However, we needed to turn to more pressing matters and find our way out, which Dagomir informed us was through the great hall.
“Before we leave, I must share something,” Ubral offered, looking at me. “The main host of the Red Riders has departed this place but some may yet remain. They are far greater than our foes the last time you were in Barovia. The captain of the force holding this keep is a vampire sorcerer of some power, known as Verik Morgain. Do not be surprised if we encounter him shortly.”
With Ubral’s warning complete, Dagomir began leading the way to the great hall and we rejoined those who had held back in the primary chamber room. Among these was Kethri, who seemed even more uncomfortable than usual at Ubral’s presence. That sparked my curiosity, as well, but we had no time to deal with such things. Eventually, Dagomir stopped in front of a set of heavy double doors, which he said led to the chapel.
As he pushed them open, we were hit with a wall of stench that dwarfed anything in Ubral’s cell. Eleven days of death and decay, although some of the corpses were clearly more recent. Some appeared to have been chewed and dragged, while other scratches seemed to match up with the oversized claw marks found in the chamber below.
Gwynlian cast a spell to speak with one of the dead, asking simply “What happened?”
“A great slaughter,” the corpse replied. “Other creatures emerged afterward, including a huge cat-like beast led by a sorcerer.”
“Any other survivors?”
“None live.”
“Any message?”
“None. Everyone I love is here.”
Gwynlian hurriedly apologized to Ubral, just as we heard a haunting voice begin to sing. A voice that Ubral seemed to recognize. As the song finished, the voice called out and echoed all round us. “Ubral, the Unbroken.Eleven days you endured. Impressive. Perhaps you endured because you changed.”
“I do not fear death,” Ubral replied.
“That is why we give you life,” the voice returned. You know nothing of what we seek. My master, Lord Karstark, has ridden to Vallaki. Erebus is not far from your trail.”
With a confidence I can only hope was genuine, Ubral said “I do not know why you torment these lands, but they are no longer undefended.”
Next, Dagomir unsheathed his sword and shards of dust floated down from the rafters above him. Before we knew what happened, something had grabbed him from above and we heard a wet, sickening crunch followed by a stream of blood falling to the floor. Ubral then cast a spell that shone like daylight to reveal a creature with unnaturally long arms and legs and an appearance that can only be described as that of a large vampiric cat. I later learned that this unholy beast is known as a vargulf. We looked on as the creature feasted on Dagomir’s corpse. Verik Morgain, the vampire sorcerer, floated into view, rising 15 feet off the ground.
As is so often the case in the blur of battle, much of the detail in what happened next is lost to me. What I remember is this: Ubral and Aldric cast spells that aided the rest of the party, while Gwynlian and Tiv combined to trap the cat-like creature within a wall of force after surrounding it with a sickening green radiance. Ismark, Kethri and I struggled to get within range of our enemies until the Verik freed the vargulf. We then focused on taking down the vile beast, with Ismark cleaving its head with his great Vorpal Sword.
Leaving only Verik, the vampiric sorcerer, our numbers were too great a match for him. A number of us dealt serious blows but it was Ismark, once again, who finished the job. Or nearly. As we stood and looked down, it was clear the sorcerer had yet an ounce of life left in him.
