
Return to Barovia, Session 5
Forgive me if my account lacks the polish of Iolas's recollections. I am not practiced at this, and I am still trying to understand what surrounds me.  Though it is somewhat cold comfort, I at least feel sure that, should The Clenched Hand try to follow me into this place, their hunt for me would soon turn to an attempt to merely survive.  That said, here is what I understand of the past little while.

The vampire sorcerer Vardik lay broken before us, clinging to what remained of his existence. The others began their questions. My mind was slower to leave the battle behind. I longed to finish what we had started, preferably with my own bare hands around this creature’s neck, but the talking had already begun.
Vardik spoke of his master, Lord Karstark, and of two brothers: Erebus, whom we had heard named before, and a third called Lord Usheron. He claimed the brothers served the same cause despite their squabbles. Then he asked to die.
But Tiv had other ideas. The small one began drawing chalk circles and speaking of souls and reincarnation, and of ways to ensure a permanent end rather than allowing the vampire's spirit to find another body. I could not follow all of it, though my eyes could follow the black blood spreading from Vardik's wounds, tendrils reaching toward the corpses scattered through the chamber.
And then, the dead rose. Not as individuals, but as a single mass, a clot of bodies fused together, lurching upward.
I moved to meet it. Or, perhaps, I should say “them”. The poison of its presence burned through me, though I pushed the pain aside. I grappled, struck, attempted to stun, but found this thing was beyond such techniques. It hit back. Hard.
The others fought well. Tiv turned the dying Vardik into his puppet and commanded him to call off his creation. Somehow this former creature of darkness still had the will to refuse, so Tiv made him fight it instead. There is something unsettling about the curious one's methods, but I cannot argue with their effectiveness.
After a viscous struggle, the clot fell.  As it did so, however, iit exploded.
I remember the spray of bone and rusted metal that pierced my body, and then I remember the  approaching floor.
I do not remember Ubral laying his hands to revive me, but they were the first thing I noticed when I came to. His magic pulled me back. I am told I should be grateful.  I am not.  My trust in this one, despite the almost worshipful way some of my companions gaze upon him, remains low.

Ubral urged us toward Vallaki, speaking of a chapel with a healer who could tend his wounds. Tiv wished to stay and search the ruins for intelligence - letters, plans, anything useful. Some in the party grew frustrated with his curiosity, feeling that it had summoned the clot that put me on the ground. I said nothing, thinking the information could prove valuable.
There was argument about our exit. Ubral pressed for the front gate, which required raising a portcullis. I wanted to go back the way we came.  I admit, something about his insistence made me push all the harder in the other direction. But Iolas reminded me we needed to stay together. I trust his judgment more than my own in this place, so I relented.
The portcullis proved difficult. Ismark managed it, though not without injury. We knew the noise may well have drawn attention we did not want, so we moved quickly.
Tiv conjured phantom steeds for the journey. Ubral rode one, looking weaker by the hour. Whatever recovery he had shown when he reclaimed his armor had faded. I asked him directly if he intended to turn on us.
"I will say no more," he said.
This did little to reassure me and only fed my distrust.  This man will bring us trouble.  I know it.

During the ride, I pulled Tiv aside.
"Little curious one. I know you do not share history with this Ubral. Do you sense what I sense? Can you do your tricks on him if need be?"
He studied me with those quick eyes, then did something, some arcane trick I did not recognize. He sensed something, though he could not name it. I told him he would have my strength if we needed to move on the cleric.
"Though it's strange," Tiv said. "He's still in favor with his god."
I do not know what to make of that. A man can serve a god and still be a danger. I have seen enough to know that much.

The others spoke of the past. Iolas and Ismark rode ahead with Ubral trailing. I caught fragments—a sister named Ireena, a woman named Tatyana, Strahd's obsession. A battle at a  coffin shop. A chapel burning along with the orphans inside. Ismark's capture, death, resurrection. His skin made into a cloak.  Horrible images indeed, though I have come to expect nothing less from this wretched place.
Ismark asked Iolas to watch him. To tell him if whatever brought him back had twisted him into something he should not be. I understand that request better than I would like.

We reached the Tser Pool at nightfall. What had been a Vistani encampment twelve days ago was now ash and ruin. The caravans were burned. The bodies were few. Ubral had said the Vistani fled west. Gwen moved through the wreckage in silence, eventually recovering a crystal ball from the caravan of someone called Madam Eva.
We made camp. The question of sleeping arrangements surfaced, and with it, the question of trust.
I wanted to be in the tent with Ubral. To watch him through the night.
Ubral refused. He wanted only Iolas and Aldric.
Aldric tried to convince me. I looked to Iolas, who has done more to win my trust than any so far, as the little curious one has done more to win what little affection I’m able to feel (a strange feeling I had sworn off and feel in spite of myself). He assured me it would be safe. I relented again.
I do not like relenting.  It is becoming a bit of a bad habit I fear.

I arose for second watch. Iolas and I sat outside while the others slept.
Suddenly, sounds came from the pool. Wailing. An old woman's voice.
Iolas recognized it, or thought he did. He woke Aldric. I woke Gwen. Then I woke everyone else. Whatever was out there, I wanted bodies ready.
Iolas and Gwen approached the water. From my position, I could hear but not see. The wailing stopped. Words were exchanged. After a time, they returned and Iolas shared what they had learned.
The voice belonged to Madam Eva, or what was left of her. A banshee, bound to the pool by a witch named Verona who had stolen her life. Eva spoke of the vampire lords serving a greater power, one that seeks to break Barovia's barrier and enter the realms beyond. They search for something in the libraries of Vallaki—something hidden long ago in the crypts of Ravenloft.
She also warned us about someone called Aenar. "Do not trust him," she said. He may have helped release the ancient power the witch serves.
Iolas found something else at the grave of a Vistani leader named Stanameer. A sword, wrapped in wax cloth. He dug it from the earth himself. There is history between Iolas and these people that I do not fully understand, but the weight of it is visible on him.

In the morning, Iolas told us everything.
We have a direction now. Vallaki and its libraries. A witch to kill if we wish to free the banshee. Brothers to face. Something ancient that everyone seems to want.
I have no stake in this land's past. But I am here, and these people, at least some of them, have earned my attention if not yet my trust.
That will have to be enough for now.
