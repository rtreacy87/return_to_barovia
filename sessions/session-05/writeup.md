# The Clot, the Portcullis, and the Pool
*Being the fifth entry in the field record of Tivwick Bramblequill, Scholar of Candlekeep*

**In-game date:** 12 Uktar, 1498 DR (estimated, `TODO(verify)`) · **Elapsed:** Day 1 since arrival in Barovia (ends at nightfall) · **Location:** [[castle-ravenloft]] → [[tser-pool]] · Route: Ravenloft great hall → portcullis → road → [[tser-pool]]

> *Reconstructed from Kethri's written recap ([[session-05/player_recap_kethri]]). Her account of my own actions is more flattering than I deserve, and I have been honest in adjusting where I can.*

## In brief
I did the thing I always do, which is to refuse to let a good specimen go. It nearly killed Kethri. I regret that, and I would do it again.

## Vardak
[[vardak]], the vampire sorcerer, lay broken before us, clinging to what was left of his existence. The others began their questions. Kethri, I learned, would have liked to finish the matter with her bare hands, but the talking had already begun.

He told us of his master, **Lord Karstark**, and of two brothers: **Erabus**, whom we had heard named, and a third called **Lord Usheron**. He said the brothers served the same cause despite their squabbles. Then he asked to die.

That, you will understand, was an offer I could not accept in its original form. I began to draw chalk circles and speak of souls and reincarnation: I wished to ensure a *permanent* end, one in which his spirit could not simply find another body. Kethri says she could not follow all of it. She is a perceptive woman and she followed enough.

I banished the intelligent part of him: the mind, the memory, the cunning. What was left in the body was a **negative-energy spirit**, and it will not be pleased to be in the material plane. I have plans for that.

## The Clot
It happened while I was speaking. The black blood from Vardak's wounds began to spread across the floor, tendrils reaching for the corpses scattered through the chamber. And then the dead rose. Not as individuals, but as a single mass, a **clot of bodies fused together**, lurching upward.

Kethri went to meet it. The poison of its presence burned through her; she grappled, struck, tried to stun it, and found it was beyond such techniques. It hit back hard.

The others fought well. I took what remained of Vardak and made him my **puppet**, and commanded him to call off his creation. To my surprise, some part of that broken thing still had the will to refuse. So I made him fight it instead. Kethri says there is something *unsettling* in my methods. I have been told this before, and by better people.

After a vicious struggle the clot fell. As it fell, it **exploded**.

I remember the spray of bone and rusted metal. Kethri remembers it better than I do: it pierced her body, and the next thing she remembers is the floor coming up. She did not see [[ubral]] lay his hands on her. They were the first thing she noticed when she woke. His magic pulled her back. She is, she says, not grateful. I understand her better than I would like.

## An Argument About Doors
Ubral urged us toward Vallaki, where there is a chapel with a healer who could tend his wounds. I wished to stay and search the ruins for intelligence: letters, plans, anything. I will confess that the party was divided. Some felt my curiosity had *summoned* the clot that put Kethri on the ground. Kethri said nothing. She thought the information might prove valuable. (This is, I think, the kindest thing anyone has done for me in a week.)

We argued about our exit. Ubral pressed for the front gate, which meant raising a portcullis. Kethri wanted to go back the way we came, and admits that something in Ubral's insistence made her push harder in the other direction. Iolas reminded her we needed to stay together; she trusts his judgement more than her own, and relented.

The portcullis was difficult. [[ismakr-der-kleiner]] raised it, at the cost of an injury. We knew the noise might draw unwanted attention, so we moved quickly.

I conjured **Phantom Steeds** for the road. Ubral rode one, looking weaker by the hour. Whatever recovery he had shown when he reclaimed his armour had faded. Kethri asked him directly whether he meant to turn on us. *"I will say no more,"* he said. That did not reassure her.

## What She Asked Me
During the ride Kethri pulled me aside. *"Little curious one. I know you do not share history with this Ubral. Do you sense what I sense? Can you do your tricks on him if need be?"*

I studied her. I did something quiet and professional, an arcane trick I will not annotate here (`TODO(verify)`: which spell). I sensed something, though I could not name it. She told me I would have her strength if we needed to move on the cleric.

*"Though it's strange,"* I said, *"he's still in favor with his god."*

I record this because it is the first indication that Kethri's distrust and my curiosity are aimed at the same man, from very different directions.

## The Road of Old Stories
The others spoke of the past, Iolas and Ismark riding ahead with Ubral trailing. Kethri caught fragments: a sister named **[[ireena]]**, a woman named **Tatyana**, **Strahd's obsession**; a battle at a coffin shop; a chapel burning with orphans inside; Ismark's capture, death and resurrection, his skin made into a cloak. Horrible images, she says. I think she is right.

Ismark asked Iolas to watch him, and to tell him if whatever brought him back had twisted him into something he should not be. I understand that request better than I would like.

## The Tser Pool
We reached the pool at nightfall. What had been a Vistani encampment twelve days earlier was **ash and ruin**. The caravans were burned. There were few bodies. Ubral had said the Vistani fled west. Gwen moved through the wreckage in silence, and eventually recovered a **crystal ball** from the caravan of someone called **[[madam-eva]]**.

We made camp, and with it came the question of who would sleep where. Kethri wanted to be in the tent with Ubral, to watch him through the night. **Ubral refused.** He wanted only Iolas and Aldric. Aldric tried to persuade her. She looked to Iolas, who assured her it would be safe. She relented again. "I do not like relenting," she writes. "It is becoming a bad habit."

She also notes that I have done more than most to win what little affection she is able to feel, a strange feeling she says she had sworn off. I am touched, and deeply curious.

## The Wailing
Kethri woke for the second watch and sat out with Iolas. Then came sounds from the pool: **wailing**, an old woman's voice. Iolas recognised it. He woke Aldric, Kethri woke Gwen, and then everyone else, because whatever was out there, she wanted bodies ready.

Iolas and Gwen approached the water. Kethri could hear from where she stood but not see. The wailing stopped. Words were exchanged. After a while they returned and Iolas told us what they had learned.

The voice was **Madam Eva**, or what was left of her: a **banshee**, bound to the pool by a witch named **[[verona]]**, who had stolen her life. Eva said that the vampire lords serve a *greater power*, one that seeks to break Barovia's barrier and enter the realms beyond. They are searching for something in **the libraries of Vallaki**, something hidden long ago in the crypts of Ravenloft. She also warned us about someone called **[[aenar]]**: *"Do not trust him."* He may have helped release the ancient power the witch serves.

Iolas had found something else at the grave of a Vistani leader named **[[stanimir]]**: a sword, wrapped in wax cloth, which he dug from the earth himself.

In the morning Iolas told us everything. We have a direction now: **Vallaki and its libraries**; a witch to kill if we wish to free the banshee; brothers to face; something ancient that everyone seems to want.

I have no stake in this land's past. But I am here.

## What I Learned
- Three brothers: **Karstark**, **Erabus**, **Usheron**. They serve a greater power.
- The vampires seek something hidden in Vallaki's libraries, taken from Ravenloft's crypts.
- Madam Eva is a banshee bound by Verona; she warned against Aenar (who, I am told, helped us in our first fight with Erabus). The warning is to be heeded.
- Ubral is still in favour with his god. Kethri distrusts him anyway.
- The Vistani fled west; their camp was burned.
