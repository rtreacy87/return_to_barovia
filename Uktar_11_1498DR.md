To Gregor, Head Archivist of Candlekeep,

11 Uktar, 1498 DR
Delivered, if the mists permit it, from Barovia.

Gregor,

You were right to send me.

I admit, when you first summoned me to review Rob’s claims, I expected another exhausted wanderer with half-understood visions and a talent for theatrical prophecy. Yet after nearly two weeks of observing the elf, I can no longer dismiss him so easily.

The man scarcely slept.

I do not mean this figuratively. During the entire time he remained within Candlekeep, I do not believe I saw him rest more than an hour at a time. Even then, he would wake muttering about “paths closing” and “the cards choosing poorly.” He would speak to almost no one except you, which I still find deeply irritating given that I am plainly the more qualified magical researcher.

Still, I understand now why you asked me to accompany the ritual and record my findings.

You sensed this was real.

And it was.

On the 11th of Uktar, Rob gathered our company within the library stacks and distributed the cards among us. The cards themselves possessed unusual magical characteristics — not merely enchanted objects, but something participatory, almost responsive. Rob instructed us where to place them within a text.

I naturally attempted to determine the nature of the ritual but was rebuffed at every turn. Rob refused to explain the significance of the cards, the text, or the ritual itself. He insisted that we simply follow his instructions and trust that the mist would answer our questions.

Infuriating man.

The moment the ritual concluded, the mists of Barovia manifested physically inside Candlekeep itself. Not an illusion. Not summoned fog. Actual Barovian mist.

Gregor, I am now almost entirely convinced the mist is sentient.

It gathered itself into the shape of a dragon composed of ash, vapor, and hostile intent so intense I could physically feel it pressing against my thoughts. The creature attacked immediately.

Yet even now I cannot determine whether the attack was punishment for failure or the challenge itself. Did we perform some aspect of the ritual incorrectly and trigger retaliation? Or was survival always the intended threshold for passage into Barovia?

The distinction matters enormously.

If the mist reacts to ritual behavior, then it possesses awareness. If it possesses awareness, then the next logical question becomes unavoidable:

What does it want?

After defeating the creature, we were transported instantly into Barovia itself — specifically onto a mountain peak where we encountered an undead figure calling himself “The Broken One.”

Remarkable specimen.

He possesses coherence, memory, identity, and self-awareness far beyond conventional undead limitations. I intend to study him further if circumstances permit. Craig, predictably, is fascinated by him and has already attempted conversation several times. To my immense surprise, the Broken One actually answers him.

I have begun developing several working theories regarding the mist and the card ritual.

My foremost conclusion remains that the mist is not merely magical, but intentional.

It categorizes. It selects. It assigns symbolic roles.

The cards distributed by Rob were as follows:

Mist — Gwynlian
Artifact — Iolas
Tempter — Kethri
Necromancer — Tivwick
Innocent — Aldric

I initially attempted a mathematical approach. The first letters of the titles produced 5! possible permutations among the current party members — one hundred and twenty combinations — or 6!, seven hundred and twenty, if the Broken One were incorporated into the pattern.

No useful result emerged.

I then calculated the full permutation space of all letters combined:

47! / (3!1!4!7!3!1!3!7!4!1!5!1!6!)

At that point I concluded I was approaching numerological delirium. Any message extracted from a permutation space that large would almost certainly be coincidence rather than intent.

No — the meaning lies in the symbolism of the assignments themselves.

And the assignments are disturbingly precise.

Gwynlian, our Vistani companion, received the Mist card despite having lost her ability to traverse the mist freely. That cannot be accidental. Either the mist seeks to explain why she lost this gift, or it intends for her to reclaim it.

This is one reason I believe the Vistani are central to understanding the true nature of Barovia’s boundaries. If anyone can communicate with the mist — assuming communication is possible — it may be them.

Iolas received the Artifact card. He carries a sword apparently connected to Barovia itself, which raises an important uncertainty: is the summoned element the blade or the bearer? I have not yet determined which possibility concerns me more.

Kethri’s Tempter card remains the most ambiguous. Is she the tempter, or the tempted? Both possibilities imply future catastrophe, merely from different directions.

As for myself — well — the Necromancer card seemed obvious initially, though the more I consider it, the stranger it becomes. Does the card refer merely to my necromantic work? Or specifically to my attempts to alter the metaphysical structure of necromancy itself through positive-energy reanimation?

If the latter, then the force behind these summons possesses an alarmingly nuanced understanding of magical theory.

I confess I find that possibility exhilarating.

Aldric, meanwhile, received the Innocent card. He is sincere, compassionate, and almost painfully earnest. Which naturally raises the question: is his purpose to preserve that innocence… or lose it?

Finally, there is the matter of Rob himself.

The monk possessed many more cards than the five he distributed among our group. Far more.

This suggests multiple groups may have been summoned into Barovia simultaneously, each carrying its own symbolic arrangement. I have also begun suspecting that an additional card in each sequence may correspond not to a summoned participant, but to a guide.

In our case, perhaps the Broken One.

If this interpretation is correct, then we must eventually determine whether these groups are intended to cooperate… or compete.

Unfortunately, Rob vanished shortly after our arrival. Though he was drawn into Barovia alongside us, he is nowhere to be found. I would greatly appreciate the opportunity to question him further, assuming he has not already been consumed by whatever metaphysical narrative machinery governs this place.

Because that, Gregor, may be the true revelation.

Barovia does not merely operate according to magical law.

It appears to operate according to symbolic law.

Stories have weight here. Meaning has structure. Narrative itself may function as a magical force capable of shaping events.

If that is true, then Barovia may represent one of the single most important arcane discoveries in recorded history.

Assuming, of course, it does not devour us first.

Please continue searching the archives for:

* Historical references to sentient mist phenomena.
* Records concerning Vistani mist traversal.
* Accounts involving symbolic summons or card rituals.
* References to entities known as “The Broken One.”
* Any evidence of magical systems governed by symbolic or narrative association.

And if Rob somehow reappears in Candlekeep before we do, I strongly advise not allowing him near the restricted stacks unsupervised. A sleep-deprived prophet carrying extradimensional summoning cards is precisely the sort of sentence that precedes archival disasters.

I await your findings eagerly.

— Tivwick Bramblequill
Scholar of Candlekeep
Researcher in Applied Positive-Energy Necromancy
